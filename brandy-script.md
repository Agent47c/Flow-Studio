## 🎙️ Complete Voiceover Script (ElevenLabs Ready)

**Voice Profile:** Male, warm storyteller with a hint of roguish wit, medium-deep baritone (135 WPM).

> "In the 1500s, shipping French wine across Europe was a merchant's nightmare: port customs taxed every single barrel by liquid volume.  
> 
> To slash their tax bill and save cargo space on wooden galleons, Dutch merchants built copper stills to boil away the water, concentrating the wine into a spirit.  
> 
> They called it *brandewijn*—literally 'burnt wine.' The plan was simple: ship the concentrate cheaply, and add water back when they reached port.  
> 
> They loaded the barrels into charred oak casks and set sail. For months, the liquid rocked in the dark belly of the ship, soaking deep into the toasted wood.  
> 
> When they docked, a merchant opened a cask to rehydrate the concentrate. But instead of clear, fiery moonshine, out poured luminous amber gold.  
> 
> Curious, he took a sip straight from the barrel. The charred oak had stripped away the harsh burn, infusing it with caramel, vanilla, and smoke.  
> 
> He shoved the water pitcher aside. Customers tasted it and begged for more. Nobody ever wanted to dilute it again.  
> 
> *Brandewijn* was Anglicized into Brandy, quickly becoming the drink of European royalty and the foundation of world-renowned Cognac.  
> 
> So the next time you savor a glass of fine brandy, remember: it was never supposed to exist—it was just a clever scheme to cheat port taxes..."
