# Complete Production Package: The Burning Stove That Saved Rubber (Vulcanization)

**Title:** The Accidental Stove Fire of Vulcanized Rubber  
**Format:** Vertical Short-Form Video (9:16)  
**Target Duration:** 65–75 Seconds  
**Audio Target:** ~155 Words (~130–140 WPM dramatic, obsessive narrative pacing)  
**Production Stack:** Flux.1 / Midjourney v6 / Google Flow (Keyframes) + Google Flow Veo 3.1 I2V (Video Motion) + ElevenLabs (Voiceover) + CapCut / Premiere Pro (Assembly)  
**Visual Style:** 1830s New England impoverished workshop aesthetic, glowing red cast-iron stove, charred elastic textures, winter blizzard vs blazing hearth contrast.

---

## 👤 Visual Character Anchor Bible (Google Flow Policy Safe)

To maintain absolute visual consistency across all keyframes and motion generation without triggering Google Flow's policy violation filters, **NO real historical names are used in prompts**. Use the persistent character anchor below:

* **Primary Character Anchor:** `A 38-year-old gaunt American inventor with deep-set hollow grey eyes, sunken cheeks, unruly wavy dark hair, wearing a worn grey wool flannel work shirt with rolled sleeves, patched brown woolen trousers, and weathered leather wrist bracers.`
* **Setting 1 (Rustic 1839 Kitchen Workshop):** `A drafty wooden New England farmhouse kitchen, frosted glass windowpanes showing falling snow outside, a glowing red-hot cast-iron potbelly stove, rustic wood shelves crowded with sulfur powders, clay jars, raw gum rubber balls.`
* **Setting 2 (Automotive / Industrial Future):** `An early 20th-century tire factory and rainy asphalt road, roaring Model T tires splashing cleanly through muddy puddles under streetlights.`

## 🎯 3-Second Retention Hook Options

* **Option 1 (Dramatic Failure - Recommended):** "In the 1830s, rubber was the laughingstock of the world—melting into stinking black sludge every summer and shattering like glass every winter."

## 🎙️ Complete Voiceover Script (ElevenLabs Ready)

**Voice Profile:** Male, deep, dramatic, gravelly documentary tone (135 WPM).

> "In the 1830s, natural rubber was considered the most useless material on earth: in summer, boots and coats melted into stinking, rotten glue.  
> 
> And in winter, it froze stiff as iron and shattered into pieces when dropped on stone. Millions of dollars vanished in bankrupt rubber companies.  
> 
> Charles Goodyear spent years in debtor's prison, selling his family's furniture to buy raw rubber and sulfur, determined to stabilize it.  
> 
> In 1839, while arguing in his kitchen, his hand slipped. A sticky ball of sulfur-treated rubber dropped directly onto the red-hot surface of a wood stove.  
> 
> He prepared for a foul fire. But instead of melting into slime, the rubber charred like leather, curing into a firm, resilient dry skin.  
> 
> When it cooled, he pulled it: it was elastic, completely waterproof, and snapped back instantly without sticking to his skin.  
> 
> He nailed a strip outside in sub-zero snow overnight. The next morning, it was still as flexible as skin. Extreme heat and sulfur had solved the riddle.  
> 
> He named the process Vulcanization after the Roman god of fire. It unlocked waterproof boots, electrical insulation, and the automobile tire.  
> 
> So the next time your tires grip the road on a rainy highway, remember: modern transportation rolled out of an accidental drop onto a burning kitchen stove..."
