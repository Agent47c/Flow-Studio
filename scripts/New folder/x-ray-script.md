```text
Did you know that X-rays were discovered when a physicist noticed a glowing paper in a pitch-black darkroom?

In 1895, German physicist Wilhelm Röntgen was testing cathode ray tubes in a completely blacked-out laboratory, wrapping the tube in thick black cardboard to block all visible light.

Suddenly, he noticed something impossible: a chemical screen sitting nine feet across the room was glowing with a ghostly green light—even though the tube was completely covered.

He placed thick books, blocks of wood, and sheets of rubber in front of it. The invisible beam passed right through solid matter as if it wasn't even there.

Having no idea what kind of mysterious energy he had unleashed, he used the mathematical symbol for the unknown: 'X.' He called them X-rays.

To prove he hadn't lost his mind, he brought his wife Anna into the lab and placed her hand over a photographic plate for fifteen minutes.

When the plate developed, they stared at the living bones of her hand and her dark wedding ring. Horrified, she cried out: 'I have seen my own death!'

He refused to patent it so doctors everywhere could use it for free. Today, X-rays save millions of human lives every single day.

So the next time a doctor takes a scan of your broken bone... did you know that X-rays were discovered when a physicist noticed a glowing paper in a pitch-black darkroom?
```
