# Complete Production Package: The Exploding Cellars of 'The Devil's Wine' (Champagne)

**Title:** The Accidental Sparkle of Champagne  
**Format:** Vertical Short-Form Video (9:16)  
**Target Duration:** 65–75 Seconds  
**Audio Target:** ~155 Words (~130–140 WPM atmospheric, suspenseful pacing)  
**Production Stack:** Flux.1 / Midjourney v6 / Google Flow (Keyframes) + Google Flow Veo 3.1 I2V (Video Motion) + ElevenLabs (Voiceover) + CapCut / Premiere Pro (Assembly)  
**Visual Style:** 17th-century atmospheric stone monastery cellars, exploding green glass shards, rising effervescent bubbles, iron fencing masks, opulent French royal banquets.

---

## 👤 Visual Character Anchor Bible (Google Flow Policy Safe)

To maintain absolute visual consistency across all keyframes and motion generation without triggering Google Flow's policy violation filters, **NO real historical names are used in prompts**. Use the persistent character anchor below:

* **Primary Character Anchor:** `A 50-year-old Benedictine monk cellar-master with a weathered kind face, grey tonsure fringe around a bald crown, intelligent gentle blue eyes, wearing a coarse dark brown wool monastic habit tied with a knotted hemp rope belt, carrying a heavy iron keyring.`
* **Setting 1 (17th-Century Abbey Wine Cellar):** `A cavernous subterranean chalk cave cellar, damp limestone walls, arched stone ceilings, endless stacked wooden racks of dusty dark green glass wine bottles, flickering iron torchlight casting long eerie shadows.`
* **Setting 2 (Royal European Ballroom):** `A gilded 18th-century royal banquet hall, crystal chandeliers, white-wigged courtiers in pastel satin brocade, golden candelabras, bubbling crystal flutes.`

* **Option 1 (Devil's Curse - Recommended):** "In the 1600s, French winemakers were terrified of bubbles—calling sparkling wine 'The Devil's Drink' because bottles kept exploding like bombs."

## 🎙️ Complete Voiceover Script (ElevenLabs Ready)

**Voice Profile:** Male, cultured, resonant, dramatic European storyteller (135 WPM).

> "In the 1600s, French winemakers were terrified of bubbles. They called sparkling wine 'The Devil's Wine' because cellars kept exploding.  
> 
> Monks literally had to wear heavy iron fencing masks into the caves just to avoid being blinded by flying glass shrapnel.  
> 
> In northern France, freezing autumn temperatures arrived early, putting the yeast to sleep before all the grape sugar turned to alcohol. They bottled it, thinking it was finished.  
> 
> When spring warmth returned, the yeast woke up inside the sealed glass, fermenting a second time and trapping carbon dioxide under massive pressure.  
> 
> One bottle would blow, triggering chain reactions that wiped out ninety percent of an abbey's entire inventory. Monks tried desperately to eliminate the bubbles.  
> 
> Until cellar masters actually tasted it. Instead of spoiled sour wine, the effervescence tickled the tongue with crisp, refreshing brilliance: 'I am drinking stars!'  
> 
> When bottles were shipped to London and Paris, royalty went wild. The fizzy defect became the most coveted status symbol in high society.  
> 
> Winemakers reinforced the bottles with thicker glass and invented wire cages to hold the corks. The Devil's Wine became Champagne.  
> 
> So the next time you pop a bottle to celebrate, remember: the world's most luxurious drink was an accidental explosion winemakers tried to cure..."
